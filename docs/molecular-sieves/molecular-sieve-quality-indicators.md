# Molecular Sieve Quality Indicators: What the Specification Numbers Mean

> **Part of the [Zeolite Molecular Sieves: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** Molecular sieve quality is defined by a handful of measurable properties: static water
adsorption (the headline capacity number), crush strength, bulk density, attrition loss and particle size
distribution. Each number guards a different failure mode — capacity guards duty, strength guards bed
integrity, attrition guards pressure-drop stability and downstream cleanliness.

## The specification sheet decoded

| Indicator | Typical range | What it protects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static water adsorption | 20–28 wt% (varies by type) | Working capacity and cycle length |
| Crush strength | 20–80 N per bead (size-dependent) | Bed integrity under load and swings |
| Bulk density | 0.6–0.8 g/mL | Reactor fill weight and logistics |
| Attrition loss | <0.5–1.0 wt% | Dust generation, ΔP stability |
| Particle size distribution | ±0.3 mm tolerance | Pressure drop and channeling control |

## Reading capacity honestly

Static water adsorption is measured at fixed conditions (e.g., 25°C, defined relative humidity) and tells
you the sieve is what it claims to be — but not what it will do in service. Working capacity in the real
gas, at real humidity and cycle time, is always lower. Quality control confirms the material; pilot data
confirms the duty.

## Strength and attrition in service

- **Crush strength matters most in tall beds** — the bottom layer carries the weight of the whole bed
  plus the force of pressure swings.
- **Attrition shows up as dust** — dust raises pressure drop, contaminates downstream equipment and
  shortens filter life.
- **Water slugs destroy both** — liquid water thermally shocks beads and cracks them; upstream knockout
  is mandatory.

## Acceptance testing in practice

A receiving inspection should verify at minimum: water adsorption on a sample, crush strength on a sample
of beads, and particle size distribution. Full pore analysis (BET, XRD) is appropriate for large fills or
when the sieve feeds a certified process.

## Manufacturer perspective

We ship every batch with its test report and recommend buyers keep the documentation — quality indicators
are also compliance evidence when the sieve serves a regulated process. Specification numbers without
