# Zeolite Molecular Sieve Guide: 5A, 13X, ZSM-5 and NaY

**Direct answer:** Zeolite molecular sieves are crystalline aluminosilicates whose
uniform pores adsorb molecules by size and polarity. For VOC adsorption concentration,
high-silica ZSM-5 (SiO₂/Al₂O₃ ≈300) is the core material; 5A and 13X serve drying and
gas separation; NaY provides very high surface area for adsorption and support roles.

## How zeolites work

The framework of SiO₄ and AlO₄ tetrahedra forms pores of fixed diameter. Molecules
smaller than the pore enter and adsorb; larger ones are excluded. Aluminum content gives
the framework negative charge compensated by cations (Na⁺ etc.) — the source of
hydrophilicity and cation-exchange behavior.

## The four types we supply

| Type | Pore class | SiO₂/Al₂O₃ | Typical role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modified 5A | ~5 Å | A-type | Drying, selective adsorption, gas separation |
| Modified 13X | ~10 Å | X-type | Drying, CO₂ / H₂S adsorption, catalyst support |
| ZSM-5 | ~5.5 Å channels | ~300 | VOC adsorption concentration (hydrophobic) |
| NaY | ~7.4 Å supercage | ~100 | High-surface-area adsorption and support |

## Why high-silica ZSM-5 for VOC

The SiO₂/Al₂O₃ ratio ≈300 makes the surface hydrophobic: water vapor — always present in
industrial exhaust — competes weakly for sites, so VOCs are captured selectively even at
60% relative humidity. High thermal and hydrothermal stability allow hot-air desorption,
which is the mechanism of adsorption concentration systems.

Documented ZSM-5 powder properties: BET ≥380 m²/g, t-plot micropore area ≥260 m²/g,
micropore volume 0.12 ml/g, relative crystallinity ≥90%, Na₂O <0.05 wt%, D50 ≤10 μm.

## Recommended operating window for ZSM-5 beds

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| VOC concentration | Low |
| Gas flow | Large |
| Relative humidity | <60% |
| Temperature | <50°C |
| Superficial velocity | ≤1 m/s |
| Bed height | ~800 mm |

## Not recommended without evaluation

Polymerizable compounds, halogens, heavy-metal salts and certain curing agents can foul
or degrade the zeolite — these streams require testing before commitment.

## Regeneration

Adsorbed VOCs desorb in hot air (typically 180–220°C for high-silica ZSM-5); the small,
concentrated desorption stream is then sent to catalytic oxidation. Cycle design
balances working capacity against regeneration energy.

## FAQ

**How long does a zeolite adsorbent last?**
Service life depends on VOC composition, humidity, regeneration temperature and cycle
count. Unlike carbon, zeolite is non-combustible and thermally regenerable, which is its
structural advantage in adsorption concentration duty.
