# Flue Gas Sampling: Getting Numbers You Can Trust

> **Part of the [Testing & Analysis: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** Flue gas measurements are only as good as the sampling behind them. Representative
sampling requires proper port placement, grid-based traverses for velocity and species, and disciplined
sample handling — condensation, adsorption losses and leaks silently corrupt results. A well-executed
sampling campaign is the foundation of every sizing and compliance decision.

## Port placement

- Locate ports where flow is fully developed — straight duct runs of 5–10 diameters upstream, 2–5
  downstream of disturbances.
- Verify by traversing: a wildly non-uniform velocity profile says the port location is wrong.
- Provide access platforms; measurements happen in the plant's reality, not the drawing.

## The traverse

A single-point sample is a guess. Standard practice is a grid traverse:

- Divide the duct cross-section into equal-area cells per the applicable method (e.g., 12–24 points).
- Measure velocity and species at each point; the report uses the flow-weighted average.
- During the traverse, watch the process: load changes mid-traverse distort the average.

## Species-specific sampling traps

- **NOx** — sample lines must stay hot to avoid NO₂ absorption; converters and calibration gases need
  checks.
- **SO₂/SO₃** — SO₃ condenses and deposits; controlled-temperature sampling is required for the SO₃
  fraction.
- **VOC** — hydrocarbon species adsorb onto unheated lines; use heated FID sample paths or sorbent
  tubes for species-level work.
- **Dust** — isokinetic sampling is mandatory; off-velocity sampling biases particle size and mass.
- **Ammonia slip** — wet-chemistry or FTIR; NH₃ adsorbs readily on surfaces, so purge and condition lines.

## Sample handling discipline

- Label every sample with time, location and process state.
- Record operating data in parallel — load, temperatures, reagent injection.
- For sorbent tubes and solutions, respect holding times and shipping temperature.
- Calibrate analyzers before and after the campaign; note both calibrations in the report.

## When to bring in outside help

Accredited third-party testing is required for compliance work in most jurisdictions — the data must
stand up to audit. In-house measurements are fine for trending, but the numbers that bind the permit
need the accredited chain.

## Manufacturer perspective

We ask for the sampling report before accepting gas data for sizing: a single-point grab sample
cannot size a catalyst. When data quality is in doubt we recommend a proper campaign first — it is
cheaper than a wrongly sized system.
