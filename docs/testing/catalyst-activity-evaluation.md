# Catalyst Activity Evaluation: From K Value to Field Verification

> **Part of the [Testing & Analysis: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** Catalyst activity is quantified as the activity constant K (m/h), derived from measured
conversion at known gas flow, temperature and composition. The K value is the universal currency of
catalyst performance — used for sizing, acceptance testing and lifecycle tracking — and it is only
meaningful when the test conditions are stated alongside the number.

## The K equation in practice

For a catalyst at area velocity AV and conversion η:

```
K = −AV × ln(1 − η)
```

A higher K means more activity per unit catalyst surface. Two catalysts with equal K values are equivalent
for sizing purposes; comparing K values measured under different gas conditions is meaningless.

## The three levels of evaluation

| Level | What happens | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laboratory rig | Sample core tested with simulated gas | Fresh-activity baseline, screening |
| Pilot / side-stream | Catalyst exposed to the real plant gas | Real-gas activity, poisoning rates |
| Field verification | Full-scale conversion + slip measurement | The guarantee: does it perform? |

## Standard test conditions

Activity tests fix the independent variables so results compare:

- Gas composition — NOx, NH₃, SO₂, O₂, H₂O, and the NO₂/NOx ratio.
- Temperature — activity is temperature-dependent; standard reference points are used.
- Space/area velocity — flow per catalyst unit.
- Sample condition — fresh, aged, regenerated; tested as cores or full modules.

## Reading activity data honestly

- A K value without gas composition is advertising, not data.
- Fresh-activity K overstates in-service performance; the design uses end-of-life K.
- Field conversion below the lab prediction usually means distribution problems or unexpected poisons —
  both are diagnosable by further testing.

## Why periodic testing pays for itself

Tracking K over time turns replacement decisions from guesswork into planning: the trend line predicts
when the catalyst crosses the end-of-life threshold, letting the plant order the replacement layer before
compliance slips.

## Manufacturer perspective

We publish K values with test conditions in writing and support customers with core sampling and
third-party laboratory verification. An activity number is a promise — it should come with the evidence
to be checked.
