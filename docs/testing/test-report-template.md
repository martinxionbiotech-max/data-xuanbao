# Field Test Report Template: The Complete Data Checklist

> **Part of the [Testing & Analysis: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** A field test report is only usable for engineering decisions when
it records the conditions, not just the headline result. A conversion number without
flow, temperature, O₂, moisture, SO₂ and catalyst volume cannot be checked, compared
or repeated. This page defines the complete field list and provides a blank template
for a customer to fill in.

## Why most field reports fail

The single most common weakness — visible across published case records, including
those in the [Evidence Registry](../methodology/evidence-registry.md) — is missing
operating conditions. A result such as "CO reduced from 1,499 ppm to 18 ppm" is
arithmetic on two numbers; it becomes engineering evidence only when the conditions
behind those numbers are recorded.

## The complete field list

| Field | Unit | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gas flow rate | Nm³/h | Sets space velocity and mass balance |
| Operating temperature | °C | Confirms the catalyst is above light-off / in window |
| O₂ concentration | % vol | Reaction stoich and CEMS normalization |
| Humidity / moisture | % or g/Nm³ | Adsorbent capacity and catalyst competition |
| SO₂ concentration | mg/Nm³ | Poison / acid-gas load |
| Dust / particulate | mg/Nm³ | Erosion and blinding risk |
| Inlet concentration (each pollutant) | ppm or mg/Nm³ | The starting point |
| Outlet concentration (each pollutant) | ppm or mg/Nm³ | The result |
| Space velocity (GHSV) | h⁻¹ | Normalizes the result to reactor size |
| Catalyst / adsorbent volume | m³ | Required to compute space velocity |
| Reactor dimensions | m | Bed geometry, face velocity |
| Continuous operating time | h | Stability, not a snapshot |
| Test instrument | model/method | Traceability and accuracy |
| Testing organization | name | Independence and method |
| Test date | date | Recency and trend baseline |

## What a report must also say about method

- **Sampling location and traverse** — where in the duct the sample was taken.
- **Analysis method** — reference method or analyzer type, calibration status.
- **Normalization** — whether values are dry, wet, at reference O₂, or as-measured.
- **Load condition** — stable load, turndown, or transient.

## How this connects to the Evidence Registry

When a field test is later cited on a product or case page, the recorded fields above
determine its **verification status** in the Evidence Registry. A test with the full
field list recorded is *Confirmed*; a test that records only inlet/outlet pairs is
*Partially recorded* and cannot be generalized beyond its stated application. The
registry applies this distinction to every documented result.

## Manufacturer perspective

We provide this checklist to customers before a trial so the test is complete the
first time. Retro-fitting missing conditions after the fact is rarely possible — the
sample is gone, the load has changed, the instrument has moved. Completeness is a
before-the-test discipline.
