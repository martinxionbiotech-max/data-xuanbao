# Sources & Evidence Levels

Technical content in this knowledge center is built on defined evidence
levels. This page explains the hierarchy and the rules for using each level.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Source type | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Company test data / official documentation | Company test reports, official product documentation, documented field test records. |
| B | Government / standards organization | National standards, ministry documents, standards-body publications. |
| C | Peer-reviewed literature / academic sources | Journal articles, academic reviews on adsorption and catalysis. |
| D | Established technical organizations | Engineering handbooks, established industry technical references. |
| E | Secondary technical source | Reputable technical media, vendor-neutral technical articles. |

## Rules

1. **No fabricated citations.** Every cited source must exist. If a source
   cannot be cited precisely, the claim is written as a qualified technical
   explanation instead of manufacturing a reference.
2. **No invented data.** Test results, capacities, certifications and
   customer names are never invented. If the data is unavailable, the page
   says what is known and what is not.
3. **No invented standards.** A standard is cited only by its real number,
   title and issuing body, and only claimed as current after verification.
4. **Company vs independent.** "Company-provided information" is
   distinguished from "independently documented information". Brochure claims
   are not automatically converted into verified facts.
5. **Qualified explanations over fake certainty.** Where evidence is
   unavailable, the knowledge center explains the general principle and marks
   the gap — it does not manufacture certainty.

## How sources appear in articles

- Level A data carries the **data classification** of the claim (see
  [Data Classification](data-classification.md)).
- Level B standards carry number, issuing body and last-verified date.
- Level C–E content is used for general mechanisms and engineering practice,
  not for company product claims.
