# Wet Scrubbers in VOC Service: What They Can and Cannot Capture

> **Part of the [VOC Treatment Engineering: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** Wet scrubbers remove VOCs only insofar as the species dissolve in the scrubbing liquid —
water-soluble VOCs (alcohols, some ketones, amines, acids) are captured reasonably well, while hydrophobic
VOCs (most solvents, aromatics, alkanes) pass through almost untouched. In VOC service the scrubber is
therefore a preconditioning or polishing device, not a destruction technology.

## The solubility boundary

Scrubber capture follows Henry's law — the equilibrium between gas-phase and dissolved pollutant:

- **Well captured** — methanol, ethanol, acetone (water-soluble), acids, ammonia, amines.
- **Partially captured** — higher alcohols, some ketones, glycols.
- **Poorly captured** — toluene, xylene, hexane, chlorinated solvents, most esters.

A "90% removal" scrubber claim always hides the species list. Against mixed printing solvent, the real
number is commonly 10–30%.

## Where scrubbers belong in a VOC train

- **Acid gas removal before catalytic oxidation** — HCl/SO₂ would poison the catalyst; the scrubber
  protects it.
- **Dust and mist removal** — overspray and sticky aerosols that would foul adsorbents.
- **Water-soluble VOC control** — when the stream is genuinely dominated by soluble species.
- **Quench after thermal oxidation** — rapid cooling of halogenated combustion products before acid
  scrubbing.

## Scrubber types in brief

- **Packed tower** — general duty, moderate pressure drop, good gas-liquid contact.
- **Venturi** — high dust/mist loads, high energy.
- **Spray tower** — low pressure drop, large flows, coarse removal.
- **Wet electrostatic precipitator** — fine mist and submicron capture where opacity limits bind.

## Design and operation cautions

- **Liquid management** — the scrubbed VOC ends up in the liquid; water treatment or disposal cost is
  part of the system.
- **Entrainment** — carryover mist wets downstream equipment; demisters and drainage are mandatory.
- **Winter operation** — freezing and crystallization risk on outdoor skids.
- **Corrosion** — absorbed species acidify the liquid; materials and pH control matter.

## Manufacturer perspective

We position scrubbers honestly: they are the best tool for acid gases, mist and soluble species, and a
poor substitute for oxidation or adsorption on hydrophobic VOCs. The species list decides — a scrubber
proposal without it is a guess.
