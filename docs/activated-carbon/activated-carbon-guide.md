# Activated Carbon Guide: Grades, Specifications and Safety

**Direct answer:** Activated carbon adsorbs organic vapors, odors and some inorganic
gases through its large internal surface (600–1,000 m²/g). Four forms serve different
duties: honeycomb (low pressure drop), columnar (durable bed media), coconut-shell
(high hardness, high purity) and fiber (fast kinetics, thin filters).

## How adsorption works

Carbon's micropore network condenses adsorbate molecules onto the surface
(physical adsorption). Capacity depends on pore volume, pore size distribution and
surface chemistry. The common quality indicators:

- **Iodine value** (mg/g) — proxy for total internal surface in the 1–2 nm range
- **CCl₄ adsorption** (%) — proxy for pore volume in the vapor-adsorption range
- **BET surface area** (m²/g) — total surface by nitrogen adsorption
- **Hardness / strength** — attrition resistance in bed service

## Form selection

| Form | Best for | Documented typical data |
|---|---|---|
| Honeycomb (SFW-10 / SFW-5) | High-flow, low-pressure-drop gas treatment | Iodine 600–900 mg/g, BET 700–1,000 m²/g, CCl₄ 45–65%, strength 1.2 MPa |
| Coal-based columnar | Bed adsorption, solvent recovery | Ø1.5–8 mm, BET ≥600–1,000 m²/g, strength ≥93–96% |
| Coconut-shell | Gas purification, food/pharma, catalyst support | High hardness, low ash |
| Fiber | Odor and low-concentration VOC filtration | Fast kinetics, thin flexible media |

## Safety limits — read this first

Carbon is **combustible**. Two risks govern industrial use:

1. **Self-heating / fire** — adsorption of ketones (MEK, cyclohexanone) and some
   unsaturated compounds releases enough heat to ignite the bed. Regeneration
   temperature, oxygen content and VOC type must be controlled.
2. **Hot desorption limit** — carbon cannot be desorbed at the 180–220°C used for
   zeolites; desorption stays limited (typically up to ~120°C in practice), which
   restricts which VOCs can be economically regenerated.

## Replacement and waste

Saturated carbon is either regenerated (where the VOC allows) or replaced. Spent
carbon is managed as waste according to its adsorbed content and local regulations —
"zero waste" claims are not made; actual classification follows the pollutant.

## Manufacturer perspective

We treat carbon and zeolite as complementary, not competing: carbon wins on capital
cost and capacity per yuan; zeolite wins on safety margin and high-temperature
regeneration. The economic answer for your plant depends on your VOC, humidity and
regeneration energy costs — we support the comparison with measured data.

## FAQ

**Why not just use carbon everywhere?**
Fire risk and regeneration limits. Ketones, humid streams and high-temperature
desorption favor zeolites; simple, dry, low-cost duties favor carbon.
