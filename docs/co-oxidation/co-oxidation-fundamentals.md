# CO Oxidation Fundamentals: Catalysts for Carbon Monoxide Removal

**Direct answer:** CO oxidation catalysts convert carbon monoxide to CO₂ via
`2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂` over precious-metal active systems on honeycomb substrates at 150–600°C,
with documented field results of 1,499→18 ppm on a sintering machine and
11,224.2→16.2 mg/Nm³ on a medical waste incinerator.

## Where CO oxidation is needed

- **Sintering machines** — large flow, fluctuating CO from incomplete coke combustion
- **Coke ovens and pellet plants** — CO in process gas
- **Waste incinerators** — CO peaks during batch feeding
- **Industrial boilers and furnaces** — CO from staged combustion
- **Internal combustion exhaust** — where applicable

## Catalyst chemistry

The catalyst provides a low-energy path for CO + O₂ recombination. Active components
involve Pt, Pd, Rh, Zr and Ce; the formulation is selected from the actual gas conditions.
The support is cordierite honeycomb or activated alumina, chosen for thermal shock
resistance (documented up to 800°C, subject to verification).

## Inhibitors

- **SO₂** adsorbs competitively; the formulation is designed for sulfur resistance but
  limits depend on concentration and temperature.
- **Water vapor** competes for active sites at lower temperatures.
- **Alkali / alkaline-earth metals** from fly ash neutralize active sites.
- **Dust** erodes and masks the surface; pre-filtration or dust-tolerant geometry applies.

## Light-off temperature

"Light-off" is the temperature where conversion reaches 50% (T50). Below light-off, CO
conversion is negligible; above it, conversion climbs steeply. Pt systems light off earlier
than base-metal oxides, which is why precious metals dominate low-temperature CO control.

## Manufacturer perspective

CO applications are rarely steady-state: sintering and incineration streams swing widely.
We size for the peak CO load with margin, not the average, and verify SO₂/water tolerance
against the actual composition. Coordination with an SCR stage (when NOx must also be
removed) affects layout — both catalysts should share gas conditioning design.

## Limitations

- CO oxidation needs oxygen; streams below ~2% O₂ require supplementary air.
- Conversion figures from original documentation are reproduced as reported and should be
  confirmed against current test reports for engineering commitments.
