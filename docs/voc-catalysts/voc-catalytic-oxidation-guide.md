# VOC Catalytic Oxidation Guide: Principles, Parameters and Practice

**Direct answer:** VOC catalytic oxidation destroys VOCs by oxidizing them to CO₂ and H₂O
over a honeycomb catalyst at 220–600°C — hundreds of degrees below thermal oxidation. Our
Pt (YC-XB-A), Pt-Pd (YC-XB-B) and Ag-Cu-Mn (YC-XB-C) catalysts target different VOC
compositions and budgets.

## Principle

The catalyst adsorbs VOC and oxygen molecules and provides an alternative reaction path
with lower activation energy. The reaction is exothermic; heat release raises bed
temperature, which is recovered in engineered systems.

```
VOC + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + heat   (over catalyst)
```

## Why catalytic beats thermal

Thermal oxidation (RTO) needs 760–850°C to destroy VOCs; catalytic oxidation achieves the
same conversion at 220–450°C. Lower temperature means less fuel, smaller heat exchanger
surface and — for dilute streams — feasibility that thermal routes lack.

## T50 / T90 / T98 explained

- **T50** — temperature for 50% conversion (light-off reference point)
- **T90 / T98** — temperatures for 90% / 98% conversion

Light-off curves are measured per VOC: aromatics, alcohols and esters each have their own
profile. Pt-Pd shifts the curve down compared with single Pt on many mixed streams.

## Poisoning and limits

- **Halogens** (chlorinated VOCs) deactivate precious metals — testing is mandatory.
- **Heavy metals and polymerizable compounds** foul the surface.
- **High humidity** competes for active sites; water-resistance verification applies.
- **Dust** erodes the washcoat; upstream filtration is standard.

## Manufacturer perspective

We classify our conversion figures as design values until the actual VOC composition is
tested. A catalyst that achieves ≥98% on toluene may behave differently on a mixed
coating-line stream — that is why we request the solvent list, concentration profile and
humidity before quoting.

## FAQ

**What is the minimum VOC concentration for catalytic oxidation?**
Below roughly 1,000–1,500 mg/m³, oxidation releases too little heat to sustain temperature
without continuous fuel input. Dilute streams are better served by adsorption
concentration first (see the VOC engineering section).
