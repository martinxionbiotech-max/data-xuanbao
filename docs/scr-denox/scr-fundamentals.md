# SCR Fundamentals: How Selective Catalytic Reduction Removes NOx

**Direct answer:** SCR (selective catalytic reduction) removes NOx from flue gas by reacting
NO and NO₂ with ammonia (NH₃) over a catalyst at 150–420°C, producing nitrogen and water.
A V-Mo-Ti catalyst system is the standard formulation for industrial applications.

## How SCR works

Ammonia is injected into the flue gas upstream of the catalyst layer. After mixing, the gas
passes through the catalyst, where the following main reactions take place:

```
4NO  + 4NH₃ + O₂ → 4N₂ + 6H₂O
2NO₂ + 4NH₃ + O₂ → 3N₂ + 6H₂O
NO + NO₂ + 2NH₃     → 2N₂ + 3H₂O   (fast SCR)
```

The catalyst lowers the activation energy so the reaction proceeds at typical flue gas
temperatures instead of the >900°C needed for thermal NOx reduction.

## Key operating parameters

| Parameter | Typical consideration |
|---|---|
| Temperature | 150–420°C for standard V-Mo-Ti systems |
| NH₃/NOx ratio | Around 0.9–1.0; excess NH₃ causes ammonia slip |
| Space velocity | Set by catalyst volume and flue gas flow |
| SO₂ content | High SO₂ raises SO₃ conversion and ammonium bisulfate risk |
| Dust loading | Determines pitch and structure (plate vs honeycomb) |

## Why temperature matters

Below the lower limit, ammonium bisulfate (NH₄HSO₄) condenses on the catalyst surface and
plugs the pores. Above the upper limit, the catalyst sinters and loses surface area. The
operating window is therefore defined by both chemistry and material stability.

## Catalyst structure basics

Two structural families dominate industrial SCR:

- **Plate-type** — metal mesh support, large pitch, high open area; preferred for high-dust
  gas where plugging and erosion are concerns.
- **Honeycomb** — extruded monolith with cell structures from 13×13 to 60×60; higher specific
  surface area (up to ~1,347 m²/m³ at 60×60), preferred for low-to-medium dust positions.

## Selection factors (manufacturer perspective)

When sizing an SCR catalyst we first collect: flue gas temperature profile, NOx in/out
targets, NH₃/NOx ratio, SO₂ and SO₃, dust loading and composition, arsenic and alkali metal
content, water vapor, pressure drop allowance and required service life. Structure and
formulation are then selected against these inputs — there is no single "best" SCR catalyst,
only the best match to a given flue gas.

## Limitations

- SO₂ in the gas is partially oxidized to SO₃; the conversion rate must stay low to avoid
  downstream corrosion and ammonium bisulfate fouling.
- Dust containing alkali metals, arsenic or phosphorus deactivates the catalyst over time.
- SCR removes NOx only; SO₂, HCl and particulate still require their own treatment stages.

## FAQ

**What NOx removal efficiency is achievable?**
The documented design efficiency of our V-Mo-Ti systems is ≥90% within the operating window.
Actual efficiency depends on temperature, NH₃/NOx ratio, space velocity and gas composition.

**Where is the SCR reactor positioned?**
High-dust (HD) layout places SCR before the precipitator; low-dust/tail-end (LD/TE) layout
places it after desulfurization. Position determines dust tolerance and temperature window.
