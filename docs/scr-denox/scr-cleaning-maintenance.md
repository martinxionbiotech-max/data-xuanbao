# SCR Catalyst Cleaning and Maintenance: Keeping the Layer Healthy

> **Part of the [SCR DeNOx: The Complete Guide](index.md)** — this article is one of the detailed pages in the guide.

**Direct answer:** In-service SCR catalyst maintenance is built around three routines: sootblowing or
acoustic cleaning to control ash deposits, pressure-drop monitoring to catch plugging early, and periodic
inspection/activity testing to time regeneration or replacement. A maintained layer keeps both conversion
and pressure drop stable; a neglected layer loses activity to plugging before chemical deactivation ever
shows up.

## Cleaning systems

- **Steam sootblowers** — the traditional answer; effective on loose ash, but condensation risk if steam
  quality is poor.
- **Acoustic horns** — low-frequency sound dislodges ash before it cakes; increasingly standard on
  high-dust applications.
- **Combined systems** — sootblowers for deep deposits, acoustic horns for continuous prevention.

Cleaning frequency is set by fuel and ash type — high-alkali fuels demand more aggressive schedules.

## Pressure drop as a diagnostic

Differential pressure across the catalyst layer is the cheapest, most reliable health signal:

- **Gradual rise** — normal ash accumulation; clean on schedule.
- **Step change** — plugging event, channel collapse or water carryover; investigate immediately.
- **No rise at all** — either unusually clean gas or bypass around the catalyst; verify sealing.

## Inspection routine

Annual or outage-based inspection covers:

- Visual face inspection for erosion, plugging and mechanical damage.
- Sample core removal for laboratory activity testing (K value).
- Hardness/compressive strength spot checks on aging modules.
- Seal and frame inspection for bypass.

## When cleaning is not enough

Cleaning restores gas paths but not catalytic activity. If inspection shows:

- Activity below the end-of-life threshold → plan [regeneration](scr-catalyst-regeneration.md) or
  [replacement](scr-catalyst-replacement.md).
- Localized erosion or collapse → replace the affected modules, not the whole layer.
- Persistent plugging from unusual fuel → revisit the cleaning system and the catalyst pitch.
